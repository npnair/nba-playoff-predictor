Offseason moves

Last season's net rating describes last season's roster. Each row adjusts it for a player who arrived, left, or returns from a long injury. Impact is points of net rating per game: positive for a gain, negative for a loss.

Injuries

Impact is how many points of net rating the team loses while the player is out. Games out counts from now. A team's rating is lowered for exactly those games in each simulated season.

Team stats

2026–27: current record and average point differential per game. History: preseason win total, last season's net rating and this preseason's record. Edits save in this browser. Export the standings to update data/standings.csv in the repo.

How it predicts

1. Build a preseason rating

Three views of each team, in points of margin per game: last season's net rating shrunk 30% toward zero plus offseason moves, the win total line converted at 2.7 wins per point, and preseason record (smoothed heavily). The sliders set the mix.

2. Blend in 2026–27 games

Once games are played, actual point differential is mixed in. With the evidence setting at 25, a team 25 games in is rated half on the preseason rating and half on its real margin.

3. Apply injuries game by game

For each remaining game, the team plays below its rating by the impact of every player still out. Opponents' injuries count too.

4. Play out and seed

Each game goes to a random opponent with home court worth 2.5 points. Teams are ranked by wins, then 7 vs 8 and 9 vs 10 play in, and the 7/8 loser hosts the 9/10 winner for the last spot.

Clinched and Out

Clinched means no more than five conference rivals can still reach this team's win total. Out means ten rivals already have more wins than it can reach. Tiebreakers are ignored.

Known overlap

The win total line already prices injuries reported before mid-October, so long absences count partly twice. Impacts are kept moderate to offset that.

Playoff bracket

Every series predicted from the standings simulation on the first tab. A series pick isn't just the higher seed: each matchup blends team strength, a position-by-position comparison of the starting fives and the two teams' games against each other this season. Click any series to see why.

How a series is picked

Team strength · 50%

Each team's rating from the standings model, after injuries expected to last into the playoffs. It's the best single predictor of who wins a series.

Starting five · 30%

Each team's five most-played healthy players, lined up point guard to center and compared slot by slot on Box Plus/Minus. Rotations shorten in the playoffs, so the starters' edge is counted at 36 minutes a night.

Season series · 20%

The two teams' games against each other this season, adjusted for home court. Three or four games is a small sample, so they're blended with team strength as if team strength were worth four more games.

Series length

The combined edge sets each game's odds, with 2.5 points of home court in a 2-2-1-1-1 format. Playing out every possible series gives the chance of each result, and the bracket shows the winner's most likely number of games.

Seeding and play-in

Seeds follow the projected standings on the first tab. Seeds 7 through 10 go through a single-game play-in decided the same way. The Finals home court goes to the team with more projected wins.

Limits

Player Box Plus/Minus comes from the box score, so it misses some defensive value. Early in the season most player numbers still lean on last season. Change the inputs on the Standings tab and the bracket updates with them.
